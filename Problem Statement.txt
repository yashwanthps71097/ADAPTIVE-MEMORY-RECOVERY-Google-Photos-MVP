## Problem Statement

Users often remember a specific old photo but cannot recall the precise details needed to retrieve it, such as the exact date, location, album, or searchable keywords.

When their initial search produces weak or irrelevant results, they often do not know **what clue to try next** and resort to repeatedly guessing different queries or give up.

The MVP should solve this specific problem:

> **How might we help users recover a remembered-but-poorly-described photo by turning incomplete memories into actionable retrieval cues and guiding them to a new retrieval path when the initial search fails?**

### Core User Scenario

A user says:

> “I’m looking for that small café we went to during our Goa trip with my friend.”

They may remember:

* Goa
* Friend
* Café
* Trip

But may not remember:

* Café name
* Exact date
* Exact location
* Album
* Searchable keywords

### MVP Goal

Build an AI-native retrieval experience with progressive multi-stage recovery:

```
        INCOMPLETE MEMORY
   “That café in Goa with my friend”
                 ↓
          UNDERSTAND MEMORY
     Extract contextual cues
   People • Place • Event • Activity
                 ↓
          INITIAL SEARCH
                 ↓
        EVALUATE RESULTS
          ┌────────┴────────┐
       Relevant             Weak
          ↓                   ↓
    PHOTO FOUND       ASK FOR MEANINGFUL
                         DETAILS
                              ↓
                 “Was it a restaurant,
                  beach café, or hotel?”
                              ↓
                   UPDATE SEARCH CUES
                              ↓
                       SEARCH AGAIN
                              ↓
                    ┌─────────┴─────────┐
                 Relevant              Still Weak
                    ↓                      ↓
              PHOTO FOUND          RECOGNITION /
                                      SUGGESTION
                                           ↓
                              “Which feels familiar?”
                                           ↓
                         Restaurant • Beach • Travel
                         Friends • Evening • Celebration
                                           ↓
                                  USER RECOGNIZES CUE
                                           ↓
                              UPDATE RETRIEVAL PATH
                                           ↓
                                    SEARCH AGAIN
                                           ↓
                                     PHOTO FOUND
```

### Core AI Intelligence

The MVP should demonstrate three AI capabilities:

**1. Understand**
Convert a vague natural-language memory into meaningful memory cues across core dimensions (People, Place, Event, Activity).

**2. Connect**
Use those cues to identify relevant photos and possible retrieval paths via multi-modal hybrid search.

**3. Recover (Progressive 2-Tier Disambiguation)**
When search confidence is insufficient, actively guide the user across a structured recovery ladder instead of asking them to guess new queries:
* **Tier 1 (First Weak Result) — Ask for Meaningful Details:** Formulate targeted clarifying questions to narrow down the setting or entity (*“Was it a restaurant, beach café, or hotel?”*).
* **Tier 2 (If Still Weak) — Recognition / Suggestion:** Surface recognizable associative cue anchors (*“Which feels familiar?”* across *Restaurant • Beach • Travel • Friends • Evening • Celebration*) to trigger episodic recognition.

### AI Engine & Inference Platform
Cognitive reasoning (understanding episodic cues and generating recovery pivots) is powered by **Groq API** (`GROQ_API_KEY`), utilizing ultra-fast LPU inference (e.g., Llama 3.3 / Llama 3.1) to ensure real-time, sub-second conversational latency.

### Key Product Principle

> **Don't make users remember more. Help them discover what they already partially remember.**

### MVP Success Criteria

A user should be able to complete a real retrieval task and successfully find the target photo, even when their initial description is incomplete.

Measure:

* Retrieval success rate
* Time to retrieve
* Number of search attempts
* Number of recovery/pivot interactions
* % of users who recognize a suggested cue
* User-reported retrieval effort
